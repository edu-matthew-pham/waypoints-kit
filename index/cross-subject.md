# Cross-subject links

Links between standards in different subjects, and threads several subjects share. A standard's prior knowledge in its own subject is in its node file (`prior_knowledge`); this page covers the rest. Made by `tools/build_index.py` from `data/cross-subject/links.json` and `threads.json`; edit those, not this page.

Timing compares the other standard's year with this one's: earlier, same year or later. Something taught later can't be assumed: teach the small piece in the lesson.

## Threads

Skill threads are ways of thinking each subject uses its own way; topic threads are content taught in more than one place. Roles: First (where students first meet it), Builds on (goes deeper), Applies (uses it in its own context), Repeats (teaches it again at about the same level, often the same year: worth coordinating).

### Water cycle (topic)

<a id="water-cycle"></a>Evaporation, condensation and precipitation moving water around Earth. Taught in Year 4 science, then met again twice in Year 7.

- First: AC9S4U02 node 2, 3, 4, 5 Water Cycle (science, Year 4). Science: evaporation, condensation and precipitation as one continuous cycle.
- Builds on: AC9S7U05 node 4 Particle Theory and Properties (science, Year 7). Science: explains evaporation and condensation with the particle model.
- Applies: AC9S7U06 node 3 Pure Substances and Mixtures (science, Year 7). Science: evaporation used to separate a mixture.
- Repeats: AC9HG7K01 node 2 Water as an environmental resource (geography, Year 7). Geography: how moving water connects places, re-teaching the cycle in the same year as science.

### Weathering, erosion and deposition (topic)

<a id="erosion-and-deposition"></a>Rock broken down, moved and laid down somewhere else. Science and Geography each teach it, Geography twice.

- First: AC9S5U02 node 1, 2 Weathering, Erosion, Transportation and Deposition (science, Year 5). Science: weathering breaks rock down; erosion, transport and deposition move it.
- Repeats: AC9HG7K01 node 4 Water as an environmental resource (geography, Year 7). Geography: moving water as a force that changes the landscape.
- Applies: AC9HH7K08 node 1 Historical and archaeological evidence (history, Year 7). History: stratigraphy, layers laid down in order, used to date finds.
- Repeats: AC9HG8K01 node 3, 4 Geomorphological processes (geography, Year 8). Geography: weathering, erosion, transport and deposition again, as landform processes.
- Builds on: AC9S8U04 node 1 Rock Cycle (science, Year 8). Science: deposited layers turn into sedimentary rock (lithification).

### Dating and deep time (topic)

<a id="dating-and-deep-time"></a>How we know how old things are, and how long Earth's history is. History uses radiocarbon dating two years before science explains half-life.

- First: AC9HH7K01 node 3 Early human migration (history, Year 7). History: radiocarbon dating and other evidence for early human migration.
- Repeats: AC9HH7K08 node 2 Historical and archaeological evidence (history, Year 7). History: dating techniques again, among the scientific methods historians use.
- Builds on: AC9S8U04 node 4 Rock Cycle (science, Year 8). Science: geological time, over millions of years.
- Builds on: AC9S9U06 node 4 Atomic Model and Radioactive Decay (science, Year 9). Science: half-life, the science behind radiocarbon dating.

### Human evolution (topic)

<a id="human-evolution"></a>Where humans came from. History meets the theories in Year 7; science explains the mechanism in Year 10.

- First: AC9HH7K01 node 1 Early human migration (history, Year 7). History: theories of human evolution and the evidence for them.
- Builds on: AC9S10U02 node 2, 3, 4 Evolution by Natural Selection (science, Year 10). Science: natural selection as the mechanism, with several lines of evidence.

### Rates (topic)

<a id="rates"></a>One quantity per unit of another. Maths teaches it in Year 8, but science uses it in Year 7 (density).

- First: AC9S7U05 node 3 Particle Theory and Properties (science, Year 7). Science: density, mass per unit of volume, a year before maths teaches rates.
- Builds on: AC9M8M05 node 1, 2 Rates (maths, Year 8). Maths: rates as comparisons of different units, in practical problems.
- Applies: AC9TDI8K01 node 2 Hardware specifications and performance (technologies, Year 7-8). Digital Technologies: speeds in hardware specifications (GHz, Mbps).
- Applies: AC9TDI8K02 node 1 Wired and wireless networks (technologies, Year 7-8). Digital Technologies: bandwidth, data per second.
- Applies: AC9TDI8K03 node 4 Representing text, image and audio (technologies, Year 7-8). Digital Technologies: sampling rate, samples per second.
- Applies: AC9S10U05 node 3 Newton's Laws of Motion (science, Year 10). Science: acceleration, change in velocity per second.
- Applies: AC9S10U07 node 5, 6 Reaction Types and Rates (science, Year 10). Science: reaction rate.

### Plate tectonics and landforms (topic)

### Evidence and claims (skill)

<a id="evidence-and-claims"></a>Telling a claim from evidence for it: fact or opinion, who says so and how they know, how far the evidence goes, and whether several independent lines support it. The biggest overlap across subjects; fact versus opinion alone is taught in four places.

- Repeats: AC9HH7S04 node 1 Describe source usefulness (history, Year 7). History: is it a factual claim or an opinion? Check it against other sources; accuracy versus usefulness.
- Builds on: AC9HH7S03 Identify source origin and purpose (history, Year 7). History: who made the source, when and why.
- Builds on: AC9HH7S07 node 1, 3 Explain historical interpretations (history, Year 7). History: fact versus interpretation; building an argument from evidence.
- First: AC9E4LA02 node 1, 2 Subjective and objective language (english, Year 4). English: fact or feeling? The language of opinion versus factual reporting.
- Builds on: AC9E7LA02 node 2 Evaluative language and substantiation (english, Year 7). English: back up a judgement with evidence from the text and other sources.
- Builds on: AC9E8LA04 node 1 Cohesion through paragraph structure (english, Year 8). English: a paragraph that makes a claim and backs it with an example, evidence and explanation.
- Repeats: AC9E8LY03 node 2 Analyse and evaluate language features and sources (english, Year 8). English: evaluate how a text uses sources and quotations.
- Applies: AC9HE7S03 node 1 Interpret Information and Data (economics, Year 7). Economics: what the data shows, a reasonable inference, or a claim that goes too far.
- Applies: AC9HC7K03 node 2 The Australian Legal System (civics, Year 7). Civics: burden of proof and evidence in court.
- Applies: AC9S7I06 node 3 Analyse methods and claims (science, Year 7-8). Science inquiry: check a claim for hidden assumptions and evidence that disagrees.
- Applies: AC9S8U03 node 6 Plate Tectonics (science, Year 8). Science: several independent lines of evidence support plate tectonics.
- Applies: AC9S10U02 node 3 Evolution by Natural Selection (science, Year 10). Science: several independent lines of evidence support evolution.
- Applies: AC9S10U03 node 5 Big Bang Theory (science, Year 10). Science: several independent lines of evidence support the big bang theory.
- Builds on: AC9M6ST02 Critique statistical arguments (maths, Year 6). Maths statistics: critique statistical arguments in the media.
- Builds on: AC9M10ST01 Critique statistical reports (maths, Year 10). Maths statistics: critique statistical reports in the media.

### Idea structures (skill)

<a id="idea-structures"></a>Common ways of organising ideas: sorting into groups and subgroups, cause and effect, time order, problem and solution, compare and contrast. English names the structures; other subjects use them.

- First: AC9E7LY04 node 1 Explain idea structures in texts (english, Year 7). English: name the ways texts organise ideas: groups and subgroups (taxonomies), cause and effect, and time order (chronology).
- Builds on: AC9E8LY04 node 1 How authors organise ideas (english, Year 8). English: add problem and solution, and compare and contrast.
- Applies: AC9S7U01 node 1, 2 Classification and Biodiversity (science, Year 7). Science: sort living things into groups and subgroups (a taxonomy).
- Applies: AC9HH7S05 node 1, 2 Describe cause and continuity (history, Year 7). History: putting events in time order (chronology), and their causes and effects.
- Applies: AC9HE7S03 node 3 Interpret Information and Data (economics, Year 7). Economics: cause-and-effect relationships in economic data.
- Applies: AC9HE7S04 Develop a Response to an Economic Issue (economics, Year 7). Economics: respond to an issue by weighing costs and benefits (problem and solution).
- Applies: AC9HG7K02 node 4 Water distribution and sustainability (geography, Year 7). Geography: strategies for water sustainability (problem and solution).

### Inquiry questions (skill)

<a id="inquiry-questions"></a>Asking a question you can actually answer by testing or researching. The four humanities subjects share one framing; science asks for investigable questions separately.

- First: AC9HS6S01 node 1, 2 Develop questions about systems (hass, Year 6). HASS Year 6: develop questions that guide every stage of an inquiry.
- Repeats: AC9HC7S01 Developing Inquiry Questions (civics, Year 7). Civics: develop a civic inquiry question, often from current events.
- Repeats: AC9HE7S01 Develop Inquiry Questions (economics, Year 7). Economics: what makes a question one you can research, using economics ideas.
- Repeats: AC9HG7S01 Develop geographical inquiry questions (geography, Year 7). Geography: ask a geographical question rather than a general one, and plan how to investigate it.
- Repeats: AC9HH7S01 Develop historical questions (history, Year 7). History: what makes a question historical, and its scope.
- Repeats: AC9S7I01 node 1, 2 Explore models and test relationships (science, Year 7-8). Science: questions you can answer by testing (investigable) versus ones you can't.

### Searching and organising information (skill)

<a id="searching-and-organising"></a>Finding sources with a search strategy, choosing what's relevant, and organising it, including choosing a table or graph.

- First: AC9HS6S02 node 1, 2, 4 Organise information and data (hass, Year 6). HASS Year 6: choose ways to collect information, judge sources, and organise it.
- Repeats: AC9HC7S02 node 2, 3 Locating and Organising Information (civics, Year 7). Civics: locate information with search strategies, then select and organise it.
- Repeats: AC9HE7S02 node 1, 2, 3 Locate and Organise Information (economics, Year 7). Economics: search with a clear aim, pick what's relevant, and organise data in a suitable format.
- Repeats: AC9HG7S02 node 2, 3 Collect, organise and represent geographical data (geography, Year 7). Geography: find secondary materials and represent data in geographical formats.
- Repeats: AC9HH7S02 node 2, 3 Locate primary and secondary sources (history, Year 7). History: find sources in archives and collections with search strategies, then sort them for the inquiry.
- Builds on: AC9M7ST02 Data Displays and Distribution (maths, Year 7). Maths: tables and graphs, and what they show about how data is spread.
- Builds on: AC9M9ST04 Choosing Data Displays (maths, Year 9). Maths: choose the right table or graph for the data and the purpose.

The ACARA Digital Literacy general capability teaches search and choosing a chart too; Kit has no node files for general capabilities.

### Classification (skill)

<a id="classification"></a>Sorting things into groups by their features: useful, with trade-offs, and open to change. All three members are in Year 7.

- First: AC9S7U01 node 1, 3, 4, 5 Classification and Biodiversity (science, Year 7). Science: why we sort living things into groups, using yes/no keys (dichotomous keys), and how the groups change over time.
- Applies: AC9HG7K01 node 1 Water as an environmental resource (geography, Year 7). Geography: classify resources as renewable, non-renewable or continuous.
- Applies: AC9M7SP04 Algorithms for Classifying Shapes (maths, Year 7). Maths: step-by-step yes/no rules (algorithms) that sort shapes.

### Logic: and, or, not (skill)

<a id="logic"></a>The same three logical operations, taught in two subjects in the same year.

- Repeats: AC9TDI8K04 node 3 Integer representation in binary (technologies, Year 7-8). Digital Technologies: logic gates (circuits that do AND, OR, NOT) and why computers use binary.
- Repeats: AC9M8P02 node 2 Combinations for Two Events (maths, Year 8). Maths: "and", "or" and "not" for combined events in probability.
