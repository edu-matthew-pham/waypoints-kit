<!-- _class: vocab -->
<!-- _header: '**Concept** Reclassification and evidence' -->

:::: grid-2x1

> **Reclassification:** Moving an organism from one group to another because new evidence shows it was in the wrong group.
>
> **Evidence:** The observations and test results scientists use to support an idea. New evidence can change which group fits best.

> **KEY VOCABULARY**
>
> **reclassification** — moving an organism to a different group
>
> **evidence** — observations and results that support an idea
>
> **DNA analysis** — examining an organism's genetic material to see how closely related it is to others

::::

<!-- Speaker note: [MINIMUM] Misconception (node glossary): reclassification means scientists 'changed their minds' or 'got it wrong'. Plant the alternative early: the system changed because the evidence changed. -->

---

<!-- _class: rules -->
<!-- _header: '**Concept** Four questions for any reclassification' -->

1. **OLD** — what was the old classification, and what was it based on?
2. **NEW EVIDENCE** — what new evidence appeared?
3. **CHANGE** — what moved, and where to?
4. **MEANING** — what does this show about how science works?

<!-- Speaker note: [MINIMUM] This scaffolds the reasoning chain the node file asks for (structured reasoning): students otherwise stop at describing what changed without saying what it means. -->

<!-- _header: '**I Do: Worked Ex. 1** The giant panda' -->

| Question | Giant panda |
|---|---|
| **Old** | Some scientists grouped it with raccoons because of similar features |
| **New evidence** | DNA analysis showed pandas are most closely related to bears |
| **Change** | Pandas were placed in the bear family |
| **Meaning** | The classification changed because the evidence changed. Science corrected itself. |

<!-- Speaker note: [MINIMUM] Narrate each row. This is the glossary's own example. For the reviewer: check the wording against a current source before use; scientists debated the panda's placement for a long time, so 'some scientists' is deliberate. -->

<!-- _header: '**We Do** ✓ Model answers' -->

| # | Model answer |
|---|---|
| **Q1** | They do not move and often grow in soil, so they looked like plants. |
| **Q2** | Fungi are not green and do not make their own food: they absorb it from what they grow on. Later DNA evidence showed fungi are more closely related to animals than to plants. |
| **Q3** | Fungi were given their own kingdom. |
| **Q4** | No. They used the evidence they had. When new evidence appeared, the classification was updated. A system that changes with evidence is working as it should. |

**"Reclassification happens when new evidence shows an organism fits better in a different group."**

> **✓ TRUE**
>
> **Why?** New evidence, such as DNA analysis, can show that an organism was in the wrong group. The classification changes because the evidence changed, which is science correcting itself, not failing.
