<!-- _class: vocab -->
<!-- _header: '**Concept** Taxonomy and kingdoms' -->

:::: grid-2x1

> **Taxonomy:** The science of naming and classifying living things into groups that fit inside each other, from very big groups to very small ones.
>
> **Kingdom:** The biggest group. We use five kingdoms: animals, plants, fungi, protists and bacteria.

> **KEY VOCABULARY**
>
> **taxonomy** — naming and classifying organisms into groups within groups
>
> **kingdom** — the biggest group an organism can belong to
>
> **observable feature** — something you can see or measure directly

::::

<!-- _header: '**Concept** Observable features' -->

We place organisms using what we can **see**, not what we assume.

| Use features like these | Not these |
|---|---|
| Green, or not green | Where it lives |
| Moves, or stays fixed in place | What it eats (unless you see it) |
| Covering: fur, feathers, scales, leaves | What it is called |
| One cell or many (needs a microscope) | "It is alive" (every organism is) |

<!-- Speaker note: [MINIMUM] Misconception (node glossary): choosing features that are not observable, or that do not separate organisms. Model the phrase 'I can see that...' and refuse answers that start 'it lives in...'. -->

---

<!-- _header: '**Concept** The five kingdoms: what we can observe' -->

| Kingdom | What we can observe |
|---|---|
| **Animals** | Take in food; usually move (some, like coral, stay fixed) |
| **Plants** | Green; fixed in place; leaves, stems or roots |
| **Fungi** | Not green; mushroom shapes or fuzzy threads; grow on or in their food |
| **Protists** | Mostly single cells that need a microscope; very varied |
| **Bacteria** | Single cells, the smallest of all; need a high-power microscope |

<!-- Speaker note: [MINIMUM] Keep this table on screen during We Do. 'Usually move' is deliberate: coral and sponges are fixed animals. Underneath, plants make their own food from light, animals take food in, and fungi absorb it from what they grow on, but students judge by what they can see. Bacteria have no nucleus, which is true but not visible with school microscopes, so it is not used here. -->

<!-- _class: rules -->
<!-- _header: '**I Do: Method** Four steps to a kingdom' -->

1. **OBSERVE** — list what you can see, not what you think it is called.
2. **MATCH** — compare your list with the kingdom table.
3. **DECIDE** — choose the kingdom the features fit best.
4. **JUSTIFY** — name one observable feature that supports your choice.

<!-- Speaker note: [MINIMUM] Say the four steps aloud, then apply them on the next slide. Students will reuse this method in We Do and You Do. -->

<!-- _header: '**I Do: Worked Ex. 1** Is a mushroom a plant?' -->

:::: grid-2x1

- **Observe:** cap and stalk, no green, grows from soil or a log
- **Match:** plants are green; this is not green
- **Decide:** fungi
- **Justify:** it is not green and it grows on its food

**Which feature is best for placing a mushroom in its kingdom?**

- **A** It grows in soil
- **B** **It is not green and it grows on its food**
- **C** It is small
- **D** It is found in forests

> **✓ Answer: B** — not being green and growing on its food points to fungi. Where it grows, how big it is and where it is found (A, C, D) do not separate fungi from plants.

<!-- _class: callout-ok verdict-false -->
<!-- _header: '**Check** ✓ Answer' -->

**"A mushroom is a plant because it grows in soil."**

> **✗ FALSE**
>
> **Why?** Where an organism grows does not decide its kingdom. A mushroom is not green and absorbs food from what it grows on, so it is a fungus.
